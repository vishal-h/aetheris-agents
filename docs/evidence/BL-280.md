# BL-280 — the amend rule's trigger was citation; it is push state

**The superseded wording**, `CLAUDE.md` §Definition of done, as it read at `35bc723`:

> **Amend a commit while it is private to the ticket and uncited; append once a packet has been
> issued against it.**

**The ruling, 2026-09-20 (the arbiter).** The trigger is **push state**. A SHA that has not been
pushed resolves for no one but the executing checkout, so a citation to it in an issued packet is
not yet a durable reference and nothing is protected by refusing to amend it. The mitigation
stands as an obligation: an amend that supersedes a SHA named in an issued packet states that SHA
in its message and says what cited it.

**The round that surfaced it, and the proximate cause.** The BL-274 fixture round. The arbiter
directed an amend of agents `722e226` — a SHA the round's first packet had cited in its
`drift_check` done-check (`current=722e226`), and which had never been pushed. The executing
session complied, mitigated by naming the superseded SHA in the amended message, and reported the
contradiction with the standing rule rather than letting the two practices diverge. The amended
commit is `35bc723`, whose message carries the mitigation verbatim: *"Amends 722e226, which this
round's first packet cited in its drift_check done-check (current=722e226). That SHA is
unreachable after this amend; the citation resolves here."* The **arbiter's instruction was the
proximate cause** — the rule as written forbade the amend, and the session followed the
instruction and filed the tension rather than resolving it itself.

**A prior round reached for push state under the old wording.** `docs/milestones/ds-t3-implementation-notes.md:284`
declined to amend not because a packet had cited the commit but because a sha had been *published*
— `f003e4a`, as a backlink on `vishal-h/aetheris#85`: *"Amending would have left a published
backlink pointing at a tree that never existed."* The reasoning is the push-state trigger, applied
under the citation wording.

**The first correction was incomplete, and the check that found it.** The rule as first landed
(agents `d98de83`, held and unpushed) stated the push-state trigger and nothing else. That trigger
protects **resolvability** — whether a cited SHA still resolves for a reader who is not the
executing checkout — and it is silent on **review integrity**, which is a separate protection:
once a packet has been issued, the bytes under review have been read, and an unprompted amendment
changes them under the reviewer. The two are independent, and a commit can be inside one and
outside the other, which is exactly the BL-274 case.

**Attribution: the R44 check, not the arbiter.** The gap surfaced from this round's own dependency
sweep, not from a separate reading. Checking what still depended on the superseded wording turned up
harness `docs/methodology/milestone-methodology.md:134` `[R44]` — *a commit held for review is pushed
by a later session, unamended* — whose conclusion the push-state-only rule left with no support
anywhere in either repo, since a held commit is unpushed and the new trigger therefore permitted
amending it. The finding is that R44 had lost its basis; the arbiter's contribution is the wording
of the clause that restores it. The rule now carries three limbs: amend while unpushed, amend a
packet-cited commit only on the arbiter's instruction, and name the superseded SHA in the message.

**What the correction dropped, and why.** The superseded entry carried three 2026-08-16 landings
as instances of appending rather than amending (`6ffcd76`; `ace771c` after `2868a3e`; `8653546`
after `900662f`). Each was decided on the citation trigger. Whether those commits were pushed at
the moment of the decision is **not established here**, so they are not carried forward as
instances of the push-state rule.
